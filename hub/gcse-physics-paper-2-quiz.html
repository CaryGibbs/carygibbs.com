The quiz

Have a go before you look at the key points. Getting some wrong is fine: trying to remember is what makes it stick, and the answers show you what to go back over.

Forces

1. Which of these is a vector quantity?

A vector has both size and direction. Velocity is speed in a given direction, so it's a vector. Speed, mass and energy have size only, so they're scalars.

Forces

2. What is the weight of a 50 kg person on Earth? (g = 9.8 N/kg)

Weight = mass × gravitational field strength (W = mg) = 50 × 9.8 = 490 N. Weight is a force, so it's measured in newtons; mass is in kilograms.

Forces

3. A 1200 kg car accelerates at 2 m/s². What is the resultant force on it?

Newton's second law: force = mass × acceleration (F = ma) = 1200 × 2 = 2400 N.

Forces

4. True or false: if the resultant force on a moving object is zero, the object stops.

False. Newton's first law: with a resultant force of zero, a moving object carries on at the same speed in the same direction. A stationary object stays still.

Forces

5. What is a car's stopping distance?

Stopping distance = thinking distance + braking distance. Tiredness, alcohol, drugs and distractions increase thinking distance; wet or icy roads and worn brakes or tyres increase braking distance. Speed increases both.

Forces

6. What does the gradient of a distance–time graph show?

The gradient of a distance–time graph is the speed. On a velocity–time graph, the gradient is the acceleration and the area under the line is the distance travelled.

Waves

7. A wave has a frequency of 50 Hz and a wavelength of 2 m. What is its speed?

Wave speed = frequency × wavelength (v = fλ) = 50 × 2 = 100 m/s.

Waves

8. Which of these is a transverse wave?

Light, like all electromagnetic waves, is transverse: the vibrations are at right angles to the direction the wave travels. Sound and ultrasound are longitudinal: the vibrations are parallel to the direction of travel.

Waves

9. Which part of the electromagnetic spectrum has the longest wavelength?

In order of decreasing wavelength (increasing frequency): radio waves, microwaves, infrared, visible light, ultraviolet, X-rays, gamma rays.

Waves

10. True or false: sound waves can travel through a vacuum.

False. Sound needs a medium (solid, liquid or gas) to travel through. Electromagnetic waves, such as light, can travel through a vacuum.

Magnetism

11. Which of these materials is attracted to a magnet?

The magnetic materials are iron, steel, nickel and cobalt. Copper and aluminium are metals but aren't magnetic.

Electromagnetism

12. How can you make an electromagnet stronger?

An electromagnet gets stronger with more turns on the coil, a bigger current, or an iron core inside the coil.

Forces: key points

  • Scalars have size only (speed, distance, mass). Vectors have size and direction (velocity, displacement, force).
  • Forces are contact (friction, air resistance, tension) or non-contact (gravity, magnetic, electrostatic).
  • Weight W = mg. Work done W = Fs (1 joule = 1 newton-metre).
  • Springs: F = ke, until the limit of proportionality.
  • Distance–time gradient = speed. Velocity–time gradient = acceleration; area underneath = distance. a = Δv ÷ t and v² − u² = 2as.
  • Newton's laws: (1) no resultant force means no change in motion; (2) F = ma; (3) every action has an equal and opposite reaction.
  • Stopping distance = thinking distance + braking distance. Typical reaction time is 0.2 to 0.9 s.
  • Higher tier: inertia, momentum (p = mv) and conservation of momentum.
  • Separate Physics only: moments, levers and gears, and pressure in fluids.

Waves: key points

  • Transverse waves vibrate at right angles to the direction of travel (light, ripples on water). Longitudinal waves vibrate parallel to it (sound).
  • Waves transfer energy, not matter. Key words: amplitude, wavelength, frequency, period (T = 1/f).
  • Wave speed v = fλ.
  • Electromagnetic spectrum (long to short wavelength): radio, microwaves, infrared, visible, ultraviolet, X-rays, gamma. All travel at the same speed in a vacuum.
  • Uses: radio for communication, microwaves for cooking and satellites, infrared for heaters and cameras, visible light for fibre optics, UV for sun tanning and energy-efficient lamps, X-rays and gamma for medical imaging and treatment.
  • Dangers: UV can cause skin cancer and premature ageing; X-rays and gamma rays are ionising and can damage cells.
  • Higher tier: refraction explained with wave fronts.
  • Separate Physics only: reflection, sound and ultrasound, seismic waves, lenses and black body radiation.

Magnetism and electromagnetism: key points

  • Like poles repel and unlike poles attract. Permanent magnets make their own field; induced magnets only become magnetic when in a field.
  • Magnetic field lines go from north to south. A compass points along the field; the Earth has its own magnetic field.
  • A current in a wire makes a magnetic field around it, stronger with more current and closer to the wire.
  • A solenoid (coil) makes a stronger field. Adding an iron core makes an electromagnet.
  • Higher tier: the motor effect (F = BIl), Fleming's left-hand rule and electric motors.
  • Separate Physics only: loudspeakers, the generator effect, and transformers.

Revising for Paper 2

Paper 2 has lots of graphs and calculations, especially in forces. A few things that tend to help:

  • Quiz first, then check the notes. Trying to remember is what makes it stick.
  • Come back to topics over several days. Little and often lasts longer than one long session.
  • Practise reading motion graphs: what the gradient and the area under the line mean.
  • Learn the EM spectrum in order, with a use and a danger for each.

Common questions

What topics are on AQA GCSE Physics Paper 2?

Topics 5 to 7: forces, waves, and magnetism and electromagnetism. Separate Physics adds topic 8, space physics. Paper 2 can also ask about ideas from Paper 1.

Is Combined Science Paper 2 the same as Physics Paper 2?

They cover the same main topics, but separate (triple) Physics has extra content. This page notes which parts are separate Physics only.

How long is the Physics Paper 2 exam?

For AQA, separate Physics Paper 2 is 1 hour 45 minutes and Combined Science: Trilogy Physics Paper 2 is 1 hour 15 minutes. Check your exam timetable for the exact dates.

Is space physics on the Combined Science paper?

No. Space physics (the solar system, the life cycle of stars, orbits and red-shift) is only in separate GCSE Physics.
