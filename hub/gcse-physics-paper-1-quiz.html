The quiz

Have a go before you look at the key points. Getting some wrong is fine: trying to remember is what makes it stick, and the answers show you what to go back over.

Energy

1. Which equation gives the kinetic energy of a moving object?

Kinetic energy = ½ × mass × speed². The others are gravitational potential energy (mgh), elastic potential energy (½ke²) and the change in thermal energy (mcΔθ).

Energy

2. What is the unit of power?

Power is measured in watts (W). 1 watt is 1 joule of energy transferred per second: power = energy transferred ÷ time.

Energy

3. A device is supplied with 200 J and transfers 50 J usefully. What is its efficiency?

Efficiency = useful output energy ÷ total input energy = 50 ÷ 200 = 0.25, or 25%. Efficiency can never be more than 1 (100%).

Energy

4. Which of these is a renewable energy resource?

Wind is renewable: it can be replenished as it's used. Coal, oil and gas are fossil fuels, which are finite and release carbon dioxide when burned.

Electricity

5. A current of 2 A flows through a 6 Ω resistor. What is the potential difference across it?

Potential difference = current × resistance (V = IR) = 2 × 6 = 12 V.

Electricity

6. True or false: in a series circuit, the current is the same at every point.

True. In a series circuit there's only one path, so the current is the same everywhere, and the potential difference is shared between the components. In parallel, the potential difference across each branch is the same.

Electricity

7. What is UK mains electricity?

UK mains is an alternating current (a.c.) supply at about 230 V and 50 Hz. Batteries supply direct current (d.c.).

Electricity

8. Which wire in a UK plug is green and yellow?

Earth is green and yellow, live is brown and neutral is blue. The earth wire is a safety wire that only carries current if there's a fault.

Particle model

9. A block has a mass of 200 g and a volume of 50 cm³. What is its density?

Density = mass ÷ volume = 200 ÷ 50 = 4 g/cm³.

Particle model

10. What is specific latent heat?

Specific latent heat is the energy needed to change the state of 1 kg of a substance without changing its temperature (E = mL). Raising 1 kg by 1 °C is specific heat capacity.

Atomic structure

11. What is an alpha particle?

An alpha particle is two protons and two neutrons, the same as a helium nucleus. It's the most ionising but least penetrating, and is stopped by paper. A beta particle is a high-speed electron; gamma is an electromagnetic wave.

Atomic structure

12. True or false: half-life is the time it takes for the number of unstable nuclei in a sample to halve.

True. It's also the time for the activity (count rate) to fall to half. Radioactive decay is random, so you can't predict when a single nucleus will decay.

Energy: key points

  • Energy stores: kinetic, gravitational potential, elastic potential, thermal, chemical, magnetic, electrostatic and nuclear.
  • Equations: Ek = ½mv², Ep = mgh, Ee = ½ke², ΔE = mcΔθ (specific heat capacity).
  • Power = energy transferred ÷ time = work done ÷ time. Measured in watts.
  • Energy can't be created or destroyed, only transferred. Wasted energy usually ends up as thermal energy in the surroundings.
  • Efficiency = useful output ÷ total input. Reduce waste with lubrication and thermal insulation.
  • Energy resources: fossil fuels and nuclear (non-renewable); wind, solar, hydro, tidal, wave, geothermal and bio-fuel (renewable). Each has pros and cons for reliability and the environment.

Electricity: key points

  • Charge Q = It. Potential difference V = IR.
  • Series: same current everywhere; potential difference shared; total resistance is the sum. Parallel: same potential difference across each branch; currents add up; total resistance is less than the smallest.
  • I–V graphs: a straight line through the origin for a resistor at constant temperature; a curve for a filament lamp; current in one direction only for a diode.
  • A thermistor's resistance falls as temperature rises. An LDR's resistance falls as light gets brighter.
  • Mains: 230 V a.c. at 50 Hz. Live is brown, neutral blue, earth green and yellow.
  • Power P = VI = I²R. Energy E = Pt = QV.
  • The National Grid uses step-up transformers to transmit at high voltage and low current, which wastes less energy; step-down transformers make it safe for homes.
  • Separate Physics only: static electricity and electric fields.

Particle model of matter: key points

  • Density ρ = m ÷ V. Solids are usually densest, gases least dense.
  • Changes of state are physical changes: mass is conserved and they can be reversed.
  • Internal energy is the total kinetic and potential energy of the particles. Heating raises the temperature or changes the state.
  • Specific latent heat E = mL: of fusion (melting) and of vaporisation (boiling).
  • Gas particles move randomly. Their collisions with the walls cause pressure. At a constant volume, higher temperature means higher pressure.
  • Separate Physics only: pressure and volume of a gas (pV = constant). Higher tier: doing work on a gas raises its temperature.

Atomic structure: key points

  • Atoms are about 1 × 10−10 m across, with a tiny nucleus of protons and neutrons, and electrons in energy levels.
  • The model changed: plum pudding, then the nuclear model after the alpha scattering experiment, then Bohr's energy levels, then Chadwick's neutron.
  • Isotopes have the same number of protons but different numbers of neutrons.
  • Alpha (helium nucleus): most ionising, stopped by paper. Beta (high-speed electron): stopped by a few mm of aluminium. Gamma (electromagnetic wave): most penetrating, reduced by thick lead or concrete.
  • Activity is measured in becquerels (Bq). Half-life is the time for the number of unstable nuclei, or the activity, to halve.
  • Contamination is radioactive atoms getting onto or into something; irradiation is being exposed to radiation from outside.
  • Separate Physics only: background radiation, uses of radiation in medicine, and nuclear fission and fusion.

Revising for Paper 1

Paper 1 has plenty of equations and calculations. A few things that tend to help:

  • Quiz first, then check the notes. Trying to remember is what makes it stick.
  • Come back to topics over several days. Little and often lasts longer than one long session.
  • Learn the equations you need to recall. Some are given on the equation sheet, but many aren't. Check which with your teacher.
  • Always show your working and units. You can pick up marks even if the final answer is wrong.

Common questions

What topics are on AQA GCSE Physics Paper 1?

Topics 1 to 4: energy, electricity, the particle model of matter, and atomic structure.

Is Combined Science Paper 1 the same as Physics Paper 1?

They cover the same main topics, but separate (triple) Physics has extra content. This page notes which parts are separate Physics only.

How long is the Physics Paper 1 exam?

For AQA, separate Physics Paper 1 is 1 hour 45 minutes and Combined Science: Trilogy Physics Paper 1 is 1 hour 15 minutes. Check your exam timetable for the exact dates.

Do I need to learn the equations?

Some of them. You get an equation sheet in the exam, but there's a list of equations you must recall from memory. Your teacher or the AQA specification will show which are which.
